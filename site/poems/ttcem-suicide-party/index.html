Suicide Party

The protagonist (X) is a manic-depressive who has experienced suicidal thoughts for most of his life. He practices meditation, but all it has served to do is create space between him and the suicidal scenarios he imagines every day. As if he were watching a movie of himself committing suicide. Being aware of the thoughts is advantageous in that at least nowadays the movies are in the third-person. X lives an average suburban life tinged with the curse of procrastination and ADD. His cluttered apartment and failing health are evidence of his pathetic stage in life. In a conversation with his best friend and roommate (Y) he describes an idea wherein he plans his own suicide and uses the money from the life insurance to throw a banger for all his friends and family.

A few weeks later, Y kills himself, and in the will he leaves instructions for his life insurance money to be spent on an elaborate and intricately planned wake. X is surprised and confused and a little hurt that his friend stole his idea. X tells his girlfriend that he's a little upset at Y for taking his idea. She blames him for planting the idea in Y's mind and breaks up with him, "I'm tired of this boring roller coaster."

Y's idea goes viral on social media. Thousands of people kill themselves in the coming week, and one-upmanship ensues.

At Y's wake, X catches up with the myriad attendees including some old friends (A, B, & C) he hadn't seen in a long time. X strikes up awkward conversations regarding suicide and the ethos of celebrating it. A says he deals with this shit by dropping acid and gives some to X. X sees his ex-girlfriend at the wake with a new person and tries to ignore them by taking the acid. Y's friends and family have a cathartic experience as the wake plays out highlights and reveals secrets from Y's life. Overwhelmed, X spends the night in the janitor's closet amused by the simple things.

As society is gripped by the trend, celebrities begin to appropriate it. Shenanigans.

Inspired by society's new, relaxed stance on suicide, and in order to fully understand Y's decision, X decides to follow through with his incessant thoughts and kill himself. As he begins to layout the plans for his own wake, X realizes he lacks any motivation to make the plans. A simple Internet search reveals Suicide Party.

Suicide Party is a startup company that offers to plan your wake for you. SP is funded by health and life insurance companies who have calculated that it would be cheaper to pay the life insurance policies of people than pay their health insurance bills.

The automated process for making an account has not yet been completed, so an in-person interview is scheduled. X is required to bring certain documents and affects to help establish the legality as well as the plans. Procrastination takes hold again and he throws together a makeshift box of trinkets to bring to the meeting.

X arrives at a shabby "office" and meets Z, a scatter-brained multi-tasker and the founder and CEO of SP. There is an initial attraction, but professionalism quickly prevails and they get down to business. They go through the paperwork and X begins to make the connection about the healthcare/life insurance companies' scheme. X is distracted by the lies he finds himself weaving for the random items he brought to inspire his wake. Z seems charmed by some of the weird things. They set a tentative date for the suicide. Z has one client ahead of X, and says they'll be in touch soon in order to finesse the details.

X wakes up the next day and feels great. He picks up a book and begins reading it, something he'd been unable to manage for years. He cooks a splendid lunch, goes for a charming walk, and takes a glorious shower. He decides to call A, B, & C, the friends he reconnected with at Y's wake, and they all meet up for drinks that night. They note X's chipper demeanor. X sees Z at a table with a group of friends and they share distant greetings. C reveals that they are planning their own wake through a company called "immOURlation," but they can't decide on the details surrounding how to actually die. B recommends a company he's been researching called "YOUicide" since they have so many more options. X becomes indignant, asking what B or C could possibly be depressed about that they might kill themselves. C is offended, reminding X "the divorce was a really painful experience." X is befuddled, "you're still engaged!" C is referring to his parents' divorce. B says he wants to die because he can't stop smoking and doesn't want to die of cancer. X is quite drunk by this time and is pointing out all the good things his friends have. It comes across as condescending, and A rises to his friends' defense. X and A have a fist fight, landing A in the hospital and X in jail.

On the TV in jail, X sees a snippet from the news. People are now throwing parties in which everyone at the party commits suicide together, and in turn they have wakes of their own wherein everyone who goes to that wake commits suicide together.

X's brother posts bail. They haven't talked in ages. "Yours is the only number I know by heart besides JG Wentworth." "Oh, you've got jokes now? Being a criminal has loosened you up, huh?" X feels bad for hurting his friend, but does feel a strange sense of glory and freedom in the experience. He's lost his job, bills are past due, electricity is turned off, is estranged from his friends (again), and he has a court date for assault and battery.

After charging his phone at a nearby hotel's free continental breakfast, X notices a voicemail from Z at Suicide Party; "I saw you in that fight at the bar. Is the guy who tripped on the bar stool okay? Why did you get arrested? BTW, convicted felons are not allowed to use the services of SP. I hope everything is okay!"

This happens, and therefore this happens, but this happens, therefore this happens.
