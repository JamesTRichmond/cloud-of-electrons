Bound(ing)

Searching for equanimity, I tip-toed carefully around the graves Noting the names of those Who sleep below the dirt That, by my toes and body motion, Is gently stirred.

I hummed softly 'til it came... FLASH! White lightning glowed. One-Mississippi, Two-Mississippi CRASH! Then down below The ground was thrashed to and fro. Yippee! I asked for rain and now, by Jove I've got it!

The sky, dark and disarranged, Slowly freed the water resting at its gates. It was time to decide. I closed my eyes. I doffed my clothes

I showered in the rain.*

Soap in hand I ran, Naked, like any other man. I was watered down, I was soaked, yet without shame.

With no disguise I hustled; Quickstepping across a street Shimmying down an ally Twisting around a corner Stomping up a hill limboing beneath lit windows.

Reeling, I stood in a field where A far off lamp was revealed and With a loco motion I made my way; I arrived at the lamp by cakewalk, by foxtrot, by paso doble.

Standing in the fashion you'd expect it to stand, the lamp Resided on a bridge painted sarcastically With a mural of kid's names Sprayed in the most splendid of ways.

The ground was broken glass and fishing string, Discarded bait and other things that People leave behind when rain-clouds gather. Their rainy day, my play date.

I lathered my hands, and under the lamp I scrubbed away my inhibitions.

I was sudded up from head to butt When a dump truck driver sped clumsily up, And passed without a wink or even a slight wave. He would have seen me if not for his task Of tilting back black cans, Gathering together the cities white trash.

I stared back at the street lamp, And at my cold breath that, for a second, Clouded the view. The light was significant, I felt invisible. A damp, yellow luminescence oil on a black and purple canvas. I was painted.† The light ruled the moment, And no one ruled the light.

Now the rain was pouring down Hard, on my crown and brow And back and frown, and on My hands and toes And skin and bones, So I began to dance.

I spun around with my arms aimed out I howled loud Teach Me Something! The raindrops were first to answer. I listened closely to every plop. With my footfall timed to every drop.

Shivering I waltzed, One big step, two small steps, Daa- da- da, Daa- da- da Until the splattering clops deafened my thoughts And I was convinced that my trot was taking me not Forward, but back To a better dance‡ When someone wanted my hand. To be held and missed To be spun and dipped, funned and kissed.

I danced through the night to the sound of the rain like Nails striking the tops of tin-cans. With my eyes closed I waltzed with my friend. Holding her tight at trunk and palm, No one led, we both followed.

We waltzed through knee deep puddles, On top of speed bumps and down the middle lane. I spun her round and she laughed aloud But when I leaned in, She bowed out.

I knew in that moment, The rain had nothing it could teach me.

The dump truck driver came by again. This time, much slower. He was old; enough so when he cranked his neck His jaw was forced to lower. "A naked man! Covered in soap! Dancing alone in the rain at two in the morning!" He was a good man, that driver. He wanted to stop, to ask if I was okay. But, not wanting to intrude, he let me dance.

Again I started trembling, Despite my constant stepping. One big one, two small ones, Daa-da-da, Daa-da-da.

I decided to do something else. Instead, I'll teach myself Since the rain won't help.

I thought to lie in the road With my mouth agape and wait Until it was full and swallow the pool.

I thought to climb a tree and jump
